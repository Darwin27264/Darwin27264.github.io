1. Why Metis (Μῆτις)? Crafting Complex Systems

In ancient Greek thought, Metis (Μῆτις) represented a profound and specific quality of mind: not abstract theory (episteme) or raw brute force (bie), but cunning resourcefulness, practical wisdom, and technical dexterity in navigating uncertain, ever-shifting environments. It was the intelligence of the navigator steering across turbulent tides, the artisan balancing metals under mechanical tension, and the strategist anticipating chaotic dynamics.

Modern computing embodies that exact reality. Building resilient distributed infrastructure, on-device AI engines, low-latency telemetry pipelines, and high-performance runtimes requires more than textbook formulas—it demands metis. It requires the practical engineering wisdom to reconcile theoretical elegance with silicon constraints, cache hierarchies, thermal limits, and real-time latency budgets.

Portfolio summaries are inherently condensed. When you inspect a featured project tile like ofln or Cognito, you see the punchy summary: "Private on-device AI chat using llama.rn & GGUF" or "Real-time OSINT dashboard in Next.js". What you don't see are the late-night memory leak investigations in C++ bindings, the battle against mobile thermal throttling, or the pipeline regex parsing that prevented schema collisions before production releases.

This archive is hosted directly in this repository to serve as a living, first-party technical notebook. No paywalls, no third-party tracking scripts, and no algorithmic noise. Just clean, rigorous architectural essays documenting real engineering decisions, benchmarks, and production lessons.

2. Core Engineering Principles

Throughout my engineering work building high-performance systems and on-device AI software, three central design tenets have consistently yielded the best outcomes:

I. Local-First & On-Device Execution

Centralized cloud APIs are powerful, but they come with high recurring costs, network latency penalties, and data privacy trade-offs. Whenever feasible, our systems prioritize local-first computation. By leveraging 4-bit and 8-bit quantized neural weights (GGUF), WebGPU in modern browsers, and SIMD hardware acceleration on ARM and x86 chips, consumer devices can execute tasks that previously required dedicated server clusters.

II. Low-Latency Over Abstraction Churn

Every layer of abstraction in modern software engineering carries a cost in latency, memory overhead, and debugging complexity. We favor systems where the execution path is transparent:

  • Direct C++ runtimes over heavy runtime wrappers when executing neural matrix math.
  • Deterministic memory management over unbounded garbage collection loops in high-throughput workloads.
  • Clean semantic code with minimal dependencies that will compile and run reliably years from today.

III. Transparent Telemetry & Observability

You cannot optimize what you do not measure. In on-device apps, we surface real-time telemetry (tokens per second, time-to-first-token latency, RSS RAM consumption). In CI/CD pipelines, we index stage-level signals to turn 50,000+ line log dumps into structured, actionable JSON summaries.

3. The Projects We Build & Dissect

The essays on this blog will dissect specific, open-source projects hosted in this ecosystem. Here is an overview of the core initiatives:

  • ofln (On-Device LLM Chat): An open-source Android and iOS mobile application that downloads GGUF models directly to device flash and chats with zero internet connection or server subscriptions. Includes multimodal vision, on-device RAG, and live inference profiling.
  • Distributed Telemetry & Log Clustering: Designing high-performance observability pipelines, automated log template clustering (Drain3), and AST-based code validation tooling for complex software workflows.
  • Cognito OSINT Intelligence: A real-time mil-spec dashboard aggregating open-source intelligence, kinetic satellite events, and maritime/aviation logistics into interactive Leaflet layers.
  • EzCap & Audio Pipelines: Mobile-friendly on-device audio captioning and video assembly using local Whisper models and FFmpeg.
